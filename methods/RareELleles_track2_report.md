# RareELleles: Track 2 methods report

MVA Hackathon 2026, Track 2 (Drug Repurposing).

## Please describe your approach in detail (how you went from variant/mechanism to candidate medication(s)).

Approach, in two arms. Arm 1, systematic screen: a protein network was built through STRING around 44 seed genes and given directed rescue logic from OmniPath; drugs were mapped to it through ChEMBL and DGIdb and annotated with Open Targets expression and safety, FAERS adverse events and ClinicalTrials.gov termination reasons; each drug was then ranked by the network proximity of its targets to the 22-gene spindle assembly checkpoint and MVA module (seed tiers A and B), against 1,000 degree-matched permutations. 2,963 drugs were ranked at STRING cutoffs 700 and 400. No candidate survived multiple-testing correction at either cutoff: 220 drugs reached nominal p below 0.05 at cutoff 700 and none reached FDR below 0.05, the lowest FDR being 0.174. The 20 records (19 drugs) that did surface below FDR 0.25 across both runs are cytotoxic chemotherapy, inhibitors of mitotic and replication kinases, and one kinase probe binding TRIP13 and BUB3; their recurring targets are POLA1, PRIM1, PRIM2, CDK1, CDK2, CDC7, CHEK1 and AURKA or AURKB, of which only POLA1 and CDC7 are in the 22-gene module. Removing CDC7, whose variant was excluded on read-level evidence, took the rows below FDR 0.25 from 17 to 13 at cutoff 700 and from 13 to none at cutoff 400. That class is the one to avoid as a corrective treatment in a chromosomal instability syndrome with heightened sensitivity to chemotherapy (PMID 39264246), so the screen is reported as a negative control rather than a source of candidates. Arm 2, allele-level reasoning: each proband variant was characterised with Ensembl VEP, UniProt domain boundaries, Exomiser, Pfam and InterPro domain analysis, AlphaMissense and GO and KEGG annotation, and classed as loss or gain of function. Candidate drugs were then filtered through four gates: market approval (ChEMBL max_phase 4); direction of effect matching the mechanism, which for BUB1B loss of function means raising BUBR1 or treating a downstream consequence and never inhibiting the checkpoint; BubR1-specific or closely related evidence; and acceptable label safety. Ranking by mechanistic fit and evidence level gave: 1 niacin (NAD+ and SIRT2 stabilisation of BUBR1); 2 gentamicin (read-through of the UGA premature stop at codon 737); 3 dasatinib (senolytic, downstream); 4 sirolimus or everolimus (mTORC1, downstream). For the other submitted genes no approved drug acting in the required direction was found: bempedoic acid inhibits ACLY, which is the wrong direction, DIDO1 and ARL9 have none, and LZTR1 and GNRHR are secondary findings. All candidates are hypotheses for testing in patient-derived cells, not treatment recommendations.

## Was candidate identification automated (e.g., via a drug-target database query or LLM-assisted search), or primarily manual literature review and expert curation?

A mixture. The network-proximity screen and the database queries (STRING, OmniPath, ChEMBL, DGIdb, Open Targets, Polly knowledge graph) are fully automated, as is the direction-of-effect and safety tiering. LLM-assisted literature search generated the mechanism hypotheses; the final candidates, their ranking and the interpretation of the screen were set by manual literature review and expert curation.

## Please describe any manual literature review or expert curation in detail.

Abstracts or full texts were read for every cited claim, and each claim is linked to a PMID or database record in the Evidence sheet of the drug table. For each candidate the review covered: approval status (max_phase, first approval, boxed-warning flag) in the Polly knowledge graph and ChEMBL; regulatory changes (ataluren: EU conditional authorisation not renewed on 28 March 2025, EMA EPAR); direction of effect against the proband's loss-of-function mechanism; BubR1-specific evidence (mouse models, MVA patient cells); label warnings and paediatric labelling. Inhibitors of BUB1, TRIP13, MPS1/TTK and Aurora kinases were excluded because they would further weaken a checkpoint that is already deficient; PLK1 inhibitors were excluded as anti-mitotic agents, and NAMPT inhibitors because they lower NAD+, the opposite of the niacin rationale (PMID 26297909); CDC20 inhibitors were excluded as not approved. The screen output was reviewed the same way: its lowest-FDR rows sit at the permutation floor and therefore share a single FDR value rather than forming a graded ranking, and two of them illustrate why target overlap alone cannot be the filter: paclitaxel's target list includes the MVA gene MAD1L1, and CHEMBL5653589, identified as the NVP-BHG712 regioisomer, carries kinobead pull-down affinities for TRIP13 (Kd 53 nM) and BUB3 (Kd 68 nM) curated in ChEMBL (PMID 29928781), which given that biallelic TRIP13 loss itself causes MVA (PMID 28553959) makes it mechanistically concerning and excluded pending functional verification rather than a demonstrated contraindication, since binding affinity does not establish functional inhibition. CDC7 was kept out of the candidate set on read-level evidence: it is called only in the provided VCF, DeepVariant calls it reference, and all six alternate reads lie on one strand against twelve reference reads on the other, Fisher p 0.0007. Rerunning the screen without CDC7 left no drug below FDR 0.25 at cutoff 400. Two limits of the screen output were also recorded: the seed report in the STRING 400 workbook is identical to the STRING 700 one, so seed coverage is reported at 700 only, and the safety review flag is set for 2,584 of 2,963 drugs, so it was not used to rank candidates.

## How did you characterize the variant's mechanism (e.g., loss-of-function vs. gain-of-function, pathway disrupted, downstream biological consequence)?

BUB1B c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter) converts codon TTA to a TGA (UGA) stop in exon 17 of 23, upstream of the kinase domain (UniProt residues 766 to 1050), with nonsense-mediated decay predicted; c.3006T>G p.(Asn1002Lys) is a missense change inside the kinase domain (AlphaMissense 0.92). Both are loss of function. In MVA patient cells, truncating BUB1B alleles produce no transcript and kinase-domain missense proteins are degraded quickly, so total BUBR1 is low, and re-expressing BUBR1 restores the mitotic checkpoint (PMID 20516114). Pathway disrupted: the spindle assembly checkpoint. Downstream consequences: chromosome missegregation and mosaic aneuploidy, cancer predisposition, and senescence-driven progeroid features in BubR1-low mice (PMID 22048312). The mechanism class was assigned from Ensembl VEP, Exomiser, UniProt, Pfam/InterPro domain analysis and GO/KEGG annotation. The DIDO1 5' UTR splice-donor deletion links to the same axis: Dido3 loss causes early BubR1 degradation in mouse cells (PMID 17299043).

## Provide a method abstract (up to 500 words). Please include strengths and limitations of your approach as well as methodological detail.

Drug-repurposing candidates were derived from the proband's Track 1 result, a BUB1B compound-heterozygous pair (p.(Leu737Ter) and p.(Asn1002Lys)), using two arms. Methods. Arm 1 screened the spindle assembly checkpoint module systematically: STRING network expansion around 22 seed genes, OmniPath directed rescue logic, drug mapping through ChEMBL and DGIdb, Open Targets, FAERS and ClinicalTrials.gov evidence, and ranking by network proximity against 1,000 degree-matched permutations, repeated at STRING cutoffs 700 and 400. Arm 2 characterised each variant with Ensembl VEP, UniProt domains, Exomiser, Pfam and InterPro analysis and GO and KEGG annotation, then filtered approved drugs through four gates: market approval, direction of effect relative to the mechanism, strength of evidence and label safety. Results. Both BUB1B alleles act through reduced BUBR1 abundance, which weakens the checkpoint and causes mosaic aneuploidy (PMID 20516114). The systematic screen ranked 2,963 drugs and returned no candidate surviving multiple-testing correction at either cutoff: 220 reached nominal p below 0.05 and none reached FDR below 0.05, the lowest being 0.174. Loosening the network threshold surfaced more cytotoxic chemotherapy rather than different biology, and the 20 records (19 drugs) appearing below FDR 0.25 across both runs are chemotherapy, mitotic and replication kinase inhibitors and one kinase probe binding TRIP13 and BUB3, a class to avoid as a corrective treatment in a chromosomal instability syndrome with heightened chemotherapy sensitivity (PMID 39264246). Removing CDC7, a module gene whose variant was excluded on read-level evidence, took the rows below FDR 0.25 from 17 to 13 at cutoff 700 and from 13 to none at cutoff 400. That negative result is reported as a control and as the reason the proposed candidates come instead from allele-level and protein-abundance reasoning, mechanisms no target-proximity method can represent. Four approved drugs were ranked as hypotheses: niacin, because NAD+ and SIRT2 stabilise BubR1 and NMN raised its abundance in mice (PMID 24825348); gentamicin, because UGA premature stops are the most readily read through (PMID 10939566) and an intravenous course restored protein from other nonsense variants in children (PMID 35234826); dasatinib, because clearing senescent cells delays disease in BubR1-low mice (PMID 22048312); and sirolimus or everolimus, because mTORC1 hyperactivity accompanies sarcopenia in BubR1-mutant mice (PMID 31738183). Strengths: candidates follow from the proband's own alleles including the stop-codon identity, direction of effect is checked explicitly, the screen provides a negative control tested at two thresholds and with an artefact gene removed, and every claim is traceable to a PMID or database record. Limitations: no candidate drug has itself been tested in a BubR1 model, the closest result having used NMN, which is not approved; the truncating allele may leave little transcript for read-through; evidence for the senolytic and mTORC1 routes is from mice; and all candidates require testing of BUBR1 levels, checkpoint function and chromosome segregation in patient-derived cells before any clinical consideration.
